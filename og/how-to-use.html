How to use these at home

Ten minutes of the right practice beats an hour of the wrong kind. Here is the short version of what we do in session, so home practice pulls in the same direction.

How often, how long

Five to ten minutes, most days, is the target — short and frequent beats long and occasional, because the skill we are building is automatic recall, and that responds to spacing. Stop while it is still going well. If a session turns into a battle, end it early and tell us: that is diagnostic information, not a failure.

  • Warm up (1 min) — one round of Word Chains or a few tiles in Blend & Build.
  • Main practice (4–6 min) — whatever your tutor has set for this week.
  • Finish on a win (1 min) — go back to a set they find easy, on purpose.

What to say when they get stuck

The instinct is to supply the word. It is kinder in the moment and it teaches nothing — the child learns that waiting produces the answer. Prompt the process instead, and give the word only after they have had a real go.

Instead of“That says chip.”
Try“What do those two letters say together?”
Instead of“No, look — it's an a.”
Try“Check the vowel. What does it say in a closed syllable?”
Instead of“You've done this one before.”
Try“Sound it out for me, one sound at a time.”
Instead of“Nearly — guess again.”
Try“That word is catch. Why tch and not ch?”

Guessing from the first letter and the picture is the habit we are replacing. If your child says a word that starts right and ends wrong, that is the signal to slow down and go left to right through every sound.

What order to work in

These games follow the sequence we teach in: sounds before rules, rules before long words, long words before word endings. Nothing here needs to be finished before the next lesson — it is practice of something already taught, never a first meeting with new material.

  1. Short vowels and digraphs — Blend & Build, Word Chains.
  2. Spelling rules — Sound Sort (ck, tch, dge, soft c and g, FLOSS).
  3. Two-syllable words — Syllable Split.
  4. Word endings — Suffix Lab.
  5. Heart words — a few minutes alongside any of the above, all the way through.

How to tell when to move on

Move up a set when it is easy on three different days — not when it is correct once. Accuracy first, speed second: a child who is right but slow is doing exactly what they should be doing at this stage. Speed comes from repetition after accuracy, never before it.

A rough guide Above 90% first time and quick: move up. Between 70% and 90%: stay, this is the productive zone. Below 70%: drop back a set — it is not a motivation problem, the step was too big.

Make it comfortable to read

The Aa button at the top of every page changes the text size, letter spacing, line spacing, typeface and background tint, and remembers your choice on that device. Extra letter spacing genuinely helps a lot of dyslexic readers; background tint is a comfort preference some children like — try it, keep it if it helps, ignore it if it does not.

Spoken sounds use your device's own voice, so nothing is recorded or uploaded. If the voice mangles a sound, turn it off in the same panel and say the sound yourself — you are better at it than the computer is.

Privacy, cost, and what these games are not

  • No account, no data. Scores are kept in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere, so there is nothing to opt out of and nothing to delete on our side.
  • No cost, no ads. Use them whether or not your child is a client.
  • Not a programme. These are drills that support structured literacy teaching. They do not assess, diagnose, or replace lessons with a trained practitioner.

Keep it on the home screen

Open a game once and it stays available with no connection — useful in the car, on a plane, or when the wifi gives up. On an iPhone or iPad tap Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android use Install app in the browser menu. It opens like an app, without being one.

Worth asking your tutor

  • Which set should we be on this week?
  • Should we be reading these words, spelling them, or both?
  • Which heart words are we working on right now?
